We're not nice to young people in this country. Or, we're not really nice to many people in this country. But bullying is a real phenomenon that might not be American-specific but is certainly a reflection of the larger, meaner society that we find ourselves in.

It is not easy to humanize a school shooter. And frankly, I'm shocked that someone would try. But what The Drama does well is actually consider all of the factors that could drive someone to consider committing one of the most heinous acts that someone can do. Much like we're never meant to ask 'what did we do wrong?' when America is attacked - this movie refuses to let an incident like a shooting existing only at the fault of the perpetuator. 

It helps that the character is portrayed by [[Zendaya]] - a wonderful actor who emits empathy and softness. I am not sure who else they could have cast in this that would have endeared the same amount of leniency required to even consider her story. And it shows what combination of things that can wrong in a child's life: changing environments, bullying, easy-access to guns, and a society that features too much violence. Growing up post-Columbine, I remember all the things that got blamed for school shootings - metal music, violent video games, movies - and what was never considered. This movie looks at what isn't considered. 

This movie doesn't work if she goes through with it (for many reasons) but mostly because it shows that things can go right.

An unfortunate, and timely, shooting that takes the life of a fellow-student causes her to see the pain she would have caused. It caused her classmates to open themselves up just enough to let her be a person in their eyes and not just a target of ridicule. It shows what happens when we are just a bit nicer to each other. Lastly, it also shows that if we provide alternate paths for people that they may just take it. Even if they're radically different than the one they planned.

It makes sense this didn't come from an American director. The use of a British actor and character at the center also helps show how bizarre the prevalence of guns is. It is extremely telling that at her own wedding, the most her father could come up with is about his own military service and the weapon that was 'mysteriously' lost. The actual gun owner couldn't come to say anything else about his daughter.
